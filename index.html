Core / Oldest Homo Evidence (~2.8 million years ago)

Time as a Long Spike Through Earth

“If we think of time, of history, as a deep and long spike driving down into the center of the earth, into the earliest hominim life, Homo Sapiens is right up near the surface, the mere head of this spike that goes very, very long ways down.”

We begin at the deepest point of this visual metaphor: the oldest currently known Homo fossil evidence (~2.8 million years ago, Ledi-Geraru, Ethiopia).

~2.4 to 1.4 million years ago

Homo habilis

Often called one of the earliest well-documented Homo species. Associated with Oldowan stone tools and East African fossil sites.

~1.9 million to ~110,000 years ago

Homo erectus

A long-lived and widely dispersed species. Evidence suggests expansion from Africa into Eurasia, larger body size, and more advanced Acheulean tools.

~700,000 to ~200,000 years ago

Homo heidelbergensis

Frequently discussed as a probable ancestor related to later lineages including Neanderthals and Homo sapiens, depending on region and interpretation.

~400,000 to ~40,000 years ago

Homo neanderthalensis

Neanderthals lived across Eurasia, adapted to diverse climates, and exchanged genes with Homo sapiens.

~300,000 years ago to present

Homo sapiens: Near the Crust

On this core-to-crust scale, our species appears only at the very top. Most of the spike lies below us in deep time.

At the Surface

“And how bitterly ironic, Bruno said, that H. taridssimus strolls in at the end of the gaping stretch - unfathomable to the mind, so much time, H. tardissmus, aka ‘Tardie’, arrives on the scene, only to destroy everything.”

Scientific note: depths are a proportional metaphor using Earth's radius (6,371 km) mapped linearly to 2.8 million years of Homo time.
