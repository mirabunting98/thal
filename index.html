A Spike of Time Through Earth

“If we think of time, of history, as a deep and long spike driving down into the center of the earth, into the earliest hominim life, Homo Sapiens is right up near the surface, the mere head of this spike that goes very, very long ways down.”

This journey uses a linear metaphor: 2.8 million years mapped to Earth’s radius (6,371 km), from core (earliest Homo evidence) toward crust (our time).

~2.4 to 1.4 million years ago

Homo habilis

Early Homo associated with Oldowan tools and East African fossil records.

~1.9 million to ~110,000 years ago

Homo erectus

A long-lived, widely dispersed species with evidence of movement from Africa into Eurasia and stronger Acheulean technologies.

~700,000 to ~200,000 years ago

Homo heidelbergensis

A likely lineage bridge in several regions, often discussed in relation to later Neanderthal and sapiens populations.

~400,000 to ~40,000 years ago

Homo neanderthalensis

Lived in Eurasia through changing climates, made complex tools, and interbred with Homo sapiens.

~300,000 years ago to present

Homo sapiens: near the crust
